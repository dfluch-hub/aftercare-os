# AfterCare OS — Product Direction V4

## Positioning
**Recovery is enough work. Organizing it shouldn't be.**

## Product promise
AfterCare OS does not ask the user to manage another complicated app.
It gives them:
1. one next useful step
2. one simple daily plan
3. one place for the details

## Why the new navigation is stronger
### Today
Only what matters now.

### Plan
Tasks, medication, visits and questions.

### Check-in
One focused logging flow.

### More
Care instructions, caregiver handoff, 30-day journey, report and data tools.

This is materially easier to understand than exposing 5–7 equal-weight feature tabs.

## Visual direction
- warm off-white background
- deep green core brand
- white cards
- subtle mint emphasis
- restrained shadows
- no gradients except the primary hero
- no oversized illustrations
- no “medical blue” cliché
- no hospital-style UI

The product should feel closer to a calm premium consumer app than to a clinical portal.

## Commercial recommendation
Keep the first launch simple:
- one paid product: AfterCare Complete
- one-time price
- no subscription initially
- no account required
- no cloud sync initially

Do not create 3–4 pricing tiers until there is proven demand.

## Suggested initial price test
A/B test:
- $14.90 launch price
- $19.90 regular price
- later $24.90 with printable caregiver/recovery bundle

## What not to add yet
- AI symptom interpretation
- automated medical advice
- diagnosis suggestions
- medication dosage recommendations
- broad health dashboards
- social feed
- chat
- community
- account creation just for the sake of it
- 20 settings screens

Those features increase risk and complexity without strengthening the core promise.
